# Problem Statement

## Background

Road freight and logistics operations in India and comparable emerging markets
rely on large fleets of vehicles moving high-value, time-sensitive, and
temperature-controlled cargo across long corridors. A single operations centre
can be responsible for dozens of simultaneous shipments, hundreds of vehicles,
cold-chain pharmaceutical and food loads, and a constant stream of disruption
signals — weather events, port restrictions, road closures, and geopolitical
incidents.

## The Problem

Supply chain operations teams have no single system that connects disruption
signals to their live fleet. When a weather event, port restriction, or road
closure occurs, operators must manually cross-reference the disruption location
against every active shipment's route, assess whether cold-chain cargo is at
risk, identify which idle vehicles could cover the gap, and decide what to do
first — all in parallel, often under deadline pressure. A typical disruption
response can consume 30–90 minutes of manual correlation per incident, and
during that window shipments continue moving toward blocked corridors and
temperature-sensitive cargo may already be excursing outside safe limits.

## Who is Affected

Fleet operations managers and logistics coordinators responsible for 20–100+
simultaneous shipments across multi-city road networks. They monitor shipment
progress, manage driver assignments, respond to disruptions, and own delivery
SLAs for customers — often with legacy TMS tools that show vehicle locations
and shipment statuses in separate screens with no automated correlation or
recommendation layer.

## Why It Matters

The cost is direct and measurable. A single delayed pharmaceutical shipment can
result in cargo spoilage worth lakhs of rupees and regulatory non-compliance.
Missed delivery windows trigger SLA penalties. Poor fleet utilisation — idle
refrigerated vehicles sitting while temperature-controlled cargo is delayed —
wastes assets that cost ₹80–120 lakh each. Operators who can act within minutes
instead of hours prevent spoilage, reduce penalties, and recover delivery
commitments before customers notice.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Existing fleet management and TMS platforms provide tracking and status
dashboards, but they do not correlate disruption signals against active routes,
do not rank shipments by urgency or cargo sensitivity, and do not surface idle
vehicles as redeployment candidates with matching capacity and refrigeration
requirements. Operators receive raw alerts — a weather warning, a port closure
notice — but must perform all downstream reasoning manually. There is no system
that closes the loop from "a disruption has been detected" to "here is the
specific shipment you must act on first and the idle vehicle that can cover it."
